What every site gets

Four things your website has to do before it's worth paying for.

Most people looking for a roofer or plumber are on their phone, often in a hurry. Your number, your service area, and a way to book have to be one thumb away.

Load fast

Getting found

Found on Google, and in the answers people get from AI.

More homeowners now skip the search results and just ask: "Who's a good roofer in Kendall?" Google's AI answers, ChatGPT, and Gemini all pull from the same place: websites that clearly say what you do, where you do it, and why people trust you. Most contractor sites say none of that in a way a machine can read.

  • A page for every service, in every area you cover

    "Roof repair in Kendall" and "metal roofs in Coral Gables" are different searches. Each one gets a real page, not one line on a services list.

  • Code that explains your business to machines

    Behind-the-scenes markup tells Google and AI assistants your services, service area, hours, and reviews in a format they read directly.

  • Your Google profile, matched to your site

    The same name, phone, hours, and services everywhere online, so search engines trust what they find.

  • Answers to the questions customers actually ask

    Plain, specific answers about cost, timing, and process, which is exactly the kind of text AI assistants quote.

Nobody can promise an AI will name your business, and anyone who guarantees it is guessing. What I can do is remove everything that's stopping it.
